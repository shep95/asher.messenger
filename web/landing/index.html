Private messenger / Signal Protocol / pre-release

Talk from anywhere. Even nowhere.

When the internet is gone, your phone still has a radio, and so does the phone next to it. Asher relays messages phone to phone, across LoRa radios, and through gateways with a satellite modem, carrying each one until it arrives. The encryption is Signal's. The delivery is yours.

01

Encryption never needed the internet.
Delivery did.

Signal Protocol runs entirely on the device. What a server did was hand out prekeys, assign identities and hold messages for people who were offline. Asher moves those three jobs onto people and radios and leaves the cryptography exactly where it was.

  1. 1

    The same encryption

    Every message is Signal Protocol ciphertext: PQXDH to open a session, the Double Ratchet after that, and a post-quantum Kyber-1024 prekey. Asher calls the same library the Signal apps ship; it only changes where the bytes go.

  2. 2

    Your identity is your key

    No phone number, no account. Your address is a fingerprint: sixteen bytes of SHA-256 over your Signal identity key, shown as thirty-two hex characters. Nobody assigns it and nobody can take it away.

  3. 3

    A contact card instead of a directory

    Your prekey bundle travels with you as a signed card: a QR code you show someone, or a beacon your phone broadcasts to whoever is nearby. Both signatures are checked before a card is trusted, and safety numbers are compared in person.

  4. 4

    Store, carry, forward

    Every node that sees a message keeps it until it is acknowledged or expires, and hands it to each new neighbour it meets. Relays never hold keys, so a message can cross a stranger's phone without the stranger learning a thing.

The audit

Automated scanning of dependencies, secrets and configuration: cargo audit, pnpm audit, Trivy, gitleaks and the GitHub Advisory Database.

Manual review of the authentication, authorization, transport-security, IPC, file-handling, attestation and remote-control surfaces of every service and client.

Dependency patches in ringrtc, SVR2, the registration service and the chat server's Jetty. Code fixes for internal-only server paths the websocket bypassed, a registration service that accepted any caller, tokens that never expired, and an Electron IPC channel any page could reach.

Verification is stated per change. Rust, Go, JDK and TypeScript changes were compiled and tested; Android and iOS changes were reviewed but not built here, and the audit says which is which.

What the radio cannot learn

Relays hold no keys. A node that forwards a bundle never has material to open it; a test asserts the relay emits no plaintext and drops nothing it should carry.

Cards and beacons are signed. Both signatures on a contact card are checked on decode; a beacon is rejected unless the card inside it signs the beacon.

Acknowledgements are authenticated. Every message commits to a secret its receiver reveals on delivery, so nobody in the middle can forge a tick.

Everything is bounded. Per-source quotas, fragment caps, rate limits per link, a seven-day TTL and an 8 MiB carry budget. A randomized test feeds every decoder garbage and asserts none of them panic.

Still visible on the air: source and destination fingerprints, which relays need to route, and padded message sizes. Content is not.

Message

Orbit

The internet is reachable. Messages go through your server as they always have, and calls, groups and attachments work in full.

Mesh · 2 hops

A peer is reachable over radio or Wi-Fi, directly or through phones in between that cannot read the traffic. Texts, voice notes and small files go through; on the same Wi-Fi, calls do too.

Carrying

Nobody is in range yet. The message is stored, signed and ready, and will be handed to the next node that appears. It keeps trying for seven days.

Out of range

Nothing at all. You can still write; it will carry the moment a link comes back. Your conversation never changes shape.

One stack. Your name on it.

Asher is one brand of a platform that white-labels the whole Signal stack: three clients, the chat server, its satellite services and the shared library. A brand is a JSON profile that one script renders into every platform, and every brand runs against its own servers. Nothing here connects to Signal's infrastructure.
